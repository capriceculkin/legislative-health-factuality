spaCy dependency

exists
What does it receive?The cleaned sentence text. It tokenizes itself.
What is stored per word?index, text, lemma, part of speech, label, head, morphology, trailing space.
How is this part of the tree built?Left to right with a stack and a buffer. At each step a small network picks one action: shift, or attach with a label. 106 actions in English, 48 in French. Always one projective tree.
How does it look through the words? What does it see?Hashed word features (shape, prefix, suffix, lowercase) plus a 300-dimension vector, through 4 convolutional layers, each seeing one word left and right.
Which label set?English: ClearNLP (dobj, prep, pobj, nsubjpass). French: UD (obj, case, obl, nsubj:pass).
How does the result change after?It does not. The rules read the tree into lines; the tree is never edited.

spaCy compared with Stanza

exists
Differences and trade-offs?spaCy decides one step at a time: fast, but an early mistake carries forward. Stanza scores every head for every word at once and keeps the best tree: slower, usually more accurate.
Does one or the other use a transformer?Neither. spaCy (lg) uses convolutions; Stanza uses BiLSTMs plus a character language model. The transformers in the pipeline are Berkeley French and the discourse parser (both xlm-roberta-large). So French constituency has a transformer and English constituency does not.
Sequoia?Both spaCy French and Stanza French train on UD French Sequoia. In French those two votes are not independent.
Same in both languagesStanza: UD labels, same architecture, same recipe
Not the samespaCy: ClearNLP in English, UD in French

Stanza dependency

exists
What does it receive?The same cleaned text, in batches of 32. It tokenizes, expands French multiword tokens (du → de le), and does not re-split.
How is this part of the tree built?Deep biaffine (Dozat and Manning 2017): a score for "word j is the head of word i" and a second score for the label, for every pair; the best tree wins. 3 BiLSTM layers, 400 units each way; 72M parameters.
Labels?UD in both languages: nsubj, obj, obl, ccomp, advcl, acl:relcl, conj, aux, case, mark.

Constituency

exists
How is it built, for both?EN (Stanza): shift-reduce over phrases, in-order transitions. FR (Berkeley benepar_fr2): every span gets a score for each of 90 labels, and a CKY search finds the best nested set. Berkeley reads spaCy's tokens.
French vs English labels?EN Penn Treebank: S, SBAR, VP, NP, PP. FR French Treebank: SENT, Ssub, Srel, VPinf, VPpart, and VN (the verb group, which holds clitics and ne ... pas). They do not line up one to one, so there are two tree rule files.
What would constituency give me that dependency won't?Explicit phrase boundaries: where the subject and object start and end, and how far "and" reaches. Dependency already gives the roles (nsubj, obj), and a phrase can be read off the head's subtree. For predication it is mainly a check on boundaries.
Apples to apples?Not today: different algorithm, encoder and labels per language. Tested Oct 19: Berkeley's English model (benepar_en3) gives the same algorithm in both languages; English vote 109/119 on the sample, 29/36 held-out (v6 rules). The encoders still differ (T5 vs XLM-R).parity_scores_const_en3.json

Rules

changed
Input
one parse
Output
subject | verb | object, kind, negated, hedged, the rule used
What published method is this closest to?ClausIE (Del Corro and Gemulla 2013): clause-by-clause rules over a dependency parse.
How do you find the main verb?The root of the tree. When the root is a noun or adjective with is / est, the predicate is that copula phrase. Auxiliaries, not and particles join it (did not pass).
Which words have a predication?A verb or copula phrase in one of 11 positions (root, conj, advcl, ccomp, relcl, acl, xcomp, parataxis, pcomp, csubj), or any verb with its own subject.
How does nsubj, obj, verb each come out?Subject from nsubj, else inherited and marked (inh). Object from obj, else a complement, else an oblique (to the moon). Verb = head plus its chain.
Result of the labeling?stated, embedded or reported, plus the rule (main clause, condition, relative clause, what is said ...). 17 rules, first match wins.
Negated and hedged words?Negated: not, never, nobody, nothing, no longer / ne ... pas, jamais, personne, rien, plus. Hedged: would, could, might, may, probably / the conditional (-rait), peut-être. Not hedges: should, must / devrait, doit.
What gets thrown out?Questions, sentences under 3 words, and lines with neither subject nor object.
Same in English and French?Since Oct 20 every word list comes from one paired file: each meaning has an English and a French side, and a check fails if a side is missing. 16 mismatches fixed on Oct 19 (devrait was a hedge in French only; French trees had no "said that" rule; the stop list was missing 34 function words, mostly French), more on Oct 20.lexicon.py · preds.py · const_fr.py

Vote

changed
What's the order of comparison? Is one greedy?spaCy, then Stanza, then constituency. Yes: each line takes the first unused match in the next list, not the best one.
What does a match really mean?Same polarity, verbs sharing their first 4 letters, and the subject and object each sharing a content word. Loose: only 54% of agreeing pairs were worded identically (v5).
What is kept, what is left out, why?Kept: lines 2 or 3 parsers produced, in spaCy's wording. Since Oct 20 the label and hedge are the majority's. Left out: one-parser lines, sent to review (v5: English 44 review lines held 4 key lines the vote missed; French 89 held 15).
Is 2-of-3 still right given the parsing differences?English: the vote (108/119, 87% of its lines in the key) beats every single parser. French: 94/116 and 78%, against Stanza alone at 95/116 and 71%. Published work supports majority voting, but over the same kind of structure; see the comparison below.
What about just 1 parser?That is the baseline and design 2 below. Best single parser: spaCy in English, Stanza in French.

Discourse: UniRST vs RST

changed
UniRST vs RST?RST is the theory (Mann and Thompson 1988): a tree of clause-sized units (EDUs), each link with a nucleus and a satellite. UniRST (Chistova 2025) is the parser: xlm-roberta-large, 18 corpora, 629M parameters. English labels from RST-DT (42); French from ANNODIS, which is SDRT converted into trees (20).
When does it run?Only on sentences where the vote kept 2 or more lines, or that contain a speech word.
What is it receiving from above?The raw sentence text only, not the parses.
What does its output mean?EDUs with character positions, and relations between them (attribution, condition, explanation ...). Each line is placed in the EDU that holds its verb.
What can change, for us?Only stated lines: attribution to someone else → reported; condition, time, purpose, relative clause → embedded.
Found on Oct 19French attribution comes out with the opposite nucleus to English ("Le ministre a dit | que ..." NS, "The minister said | that ..." SN), so the speech verb itself was being re-labeled. Fixed Oct 20: the source side is found by its words (said, dit, according to, selon).parity_parses.json · test D1

Input is the prior parse?Yes, the backbone parse.
Output one standard frame per predicate?Built Oct 20 (frames.py): PropBank-style roles read from the UD tree with one mapping for both languages. Active subject ARG0, passive subject ARG1, object ARG1, the controlled object ARG2 (urge [the doctors] to ...), the by / par agent ARG0, time words ARGM-TMP, negation ARGM-NEG, modal or French conditional ARGM-MOD. Verbs with no ARG0 in any PropBank sense (the program failed) get ARG1 subjects, read from the English PropBank frame files in both languages.frames.py · propbank-frames
Is the mapping right? (the field's standard check)Scored against Universal Proposition Bank 1.0, on its own UD trees, so this measures the rules alone. English (PropBank roles on the English Web Treebank): 70% of verb arguments get the gold role (test and dev both 70%); ARG0 97%, ARG1 76%, ARGM-MOD 100%, ARGM-NEG 92%, ARG2 only 8%. French (roles projected from English, not hand-checked): 78% test, 79% dev; ARG0 100%, ARG1 71%. French precision is not meaningful because the projected gold misses many arguments and labels words like également as time.eval_roles.py · UP_English-EWT · UP_French
Known gapARG2 and the numbered roles above it depend on each verb's sense (give X to Y, move from X to Y). Getting them right needs sense disambiguation against the PropBank frame files, in both languages.open
Hedges are what here?Epistemic only, the same list in both languages: would, could, might, may, probably, perhaps / the conditional of any verb, peut-être, probablement. Should, must / devrait, doit are obligation, not hedges. Open policy: seems / semble, and I think / je pense.
Cause clauses?Stated: because / parce que, since / puisque. Not stated: condition, time, concession, whether (if / si, when / quand, until / jusqu'à ce que, unless / à moins que, although / bien que). Open policy: as / comme opening a clause.
How to test? How to show for both EN and FR?The 45 pairs with a pass or fail per language for each construction, then the held-out and 100-sentence keys per language, with precision as well as recall.

Discourse

measured
Built on what? Labels include? How was it trained?UniRST: xlm-roberta-large; a BiLSTM-CRF cuts the EDUs, a pointer network builds the tree, a biaffine classifier picks the relation. Trained on 18 corpora: English RST-DT (385 documents, 42 labels), French ANNODIS (86 documents, 20 labels). Published relation accuracy 55.2 English, 31.1 French.
Same labels for both?Can be: the model predicts one shared set and each corpus's labels are a mask, so French can be read with the RST-DT labels. Tested Oct 19: French sample unchanged (93/116, v6).
Should the input be the sentence or the surrounding sentences?Run Oct 20: the 130 answer-key sentences again with the sentence before and after each (129 parsed; 1 French sentence failed to align). Over the 155 key lines both keys find, the discourse step changes: one sentence, English 0 and French 1 (right); three sentences, English 0 and French 2 (both right); three sentences with the RST-DT labels for French, French 3 (2 right, 1 wrong). The window helps a little in French and never hurts English.discourse_window.py · discourse_window.json
So what does discourse add?Very little now: once the syntax rules handle attribution, conditions and time clauses the same way in both languages, the discourse step changes at most 3 of 155 labels. Recommendation: keep it for attribution only, run it on a three-sentence window, and say plainly that the syntax does most of the work.
Output is what?EDUs and relations, used to correct stated lines (mainly attribution). If it adds little over the syntax, keep it for attribution only and say so.

What the numbers say

On finding the key's lines, the vote is best in English and close to best in French. The backbone design loses English lines because Stanza English is weaker than spaCy. Using the UD structure check as a filter (2c) is too strict; as a confidence flag it is informative in both languages the same way. What design 2 adds that design 1 does not: one structure, role-labeled frames scored against a standard gold set, and a check that treats English and French alike.

What published work does

  • Henderson and Brill 1999: keep a constituent when at least 2 of 3 parsers propose it; beat every single parser.
  • Zeman and Žabokrtský 2005: vote on each word's head over 7 dependency parsers; weak parsers still help; voting needs at least 3.
  • Surdeanu and Manning 2010: simple voting did as well as complex models; the diversity of the parsers mattered most.
  • Sagae and Lavie 2006: merge the outputs into one weighted graph, then reparse it into one tree.

The gap, and design 3

All four vote on the same kind of structure (constituents, or each word's head). This pipeline votes on extracted lines across different formalisms, which none of them do. The defensible version: parsers that all output UD, a vote on each word's head and relation, a reparse into one tree, then the rules once. That needs a second UD parser for English.

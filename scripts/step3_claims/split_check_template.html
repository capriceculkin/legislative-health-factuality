Sentence break check

How to do it. Each box is one real paragraph from the debates, exactly as the pipeline received it, with no breaks shown.
  1. Read it and click the last word of every sentence. A red ‖ appears after it. Click again to remove it. The last word of the paragraph is always the end, so you don't need to mark it.
  2. Mark where a reader would say a sentence ends. A colon or semicolon is usually not a break; break there only if what follows is clearly a new sentence. A quotation that ends with a period inside the quote marks ends the sentence.
  3. Abbreviations like Mr., M., H.R., S.O. or a.m. are not breaks unless the sentence really ends there.
  4. If text is not a sentence at all (a heading, a vote count, "Some hon. Members: Hear, hear!"), tick has text that is not a sentence and still mark where it stops.
  5. Press Done on each paragraph. When all are done, Compare with the splitter shows where the pipeline's splitter agrees with you. It stays hidden until then, so your marks are your own.
  6. Your marks are kept in this browser. Press Save my marks at the end and send the file to Claude.
